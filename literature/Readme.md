# The Economics of TFM on Blockchains

Chung and Shi proposed that an ideal TFM should satisfy three properties: user incentive compatibility (UIC), miner incentive compatibility (MIC), and miner-ser side contract proofness (SCP).11 In other words, in a dreamed TFM, the
user’s optimal strategy lies in truthful bidding, the miner’s optimal strategy involves faithfully
implementing the prescribed mechanism, and neither the miner nor the users can increase their utility by deviating from honest behavior. EIP-1559 TFM transforms the traditional first-price auction TFM on Ethereum into a hy-
brid system that combines elements of both a first-price auction and a dynamic posted-price
mechanism. Roughgarden argues that by setting the base fee sufficiently high, the number of
transactions willing to bid for inclusion within a block aligns with the block size. In this case,
where a first-price auction won’t be called, it enables EIP-1559 to fulfill these three properties.
However, Chung and Shi suggest that, in practice, congestion tends to occur for various reasons,
leading to the activation of the first-price auction and, consequently, failing to satisfy the User
Incentive Compatibility (UIC) property.11 Chung and Shi argue that it is impossible to develop a
TFM to meet all three properties.

# Machine Learning, Market and Market Design

The intersection of machine learning
and blockchain has opened up new possibilities for optimizing market mechanisms and resource
allocation. Interdisciplinary researchers have tried to integrate machine learning models and
market mechanism design to reach efficient market equilibrium and resource allocation.
In the context of an online ride-hailing platform, Uber employs a dual-component mechanism
involving basic fee calculation and surge pricing. The basic fee, similar to the blockchain’s TFM,
is determined by fixed formulas tied to the base fare, minimum fare, and dollars per mile. What
distinguishes Uber from traditional taxis is its notable surge pricing mechanism, triggered to raise
the price when demand exceeds the available supply. Chao has utilized Linear Regression,
logistical regression models, and time series analysis to forecast Uber’s pricing based on factors
like location, wealth, and time. Additionally, Sindhu et.al. employ a variety of prediction
models, including linear regression, decision tree, random forest, multilayer perceptron, and
gradient boost, in an attempt to estimate Uber’s pricing.
In the realms of computing networks, Luong et al. have developed an optimal auction based
on feed-forward neural networks to better allocate fog resources in blockchain networks.17 Butler
and Crane used Direct-Recursive Hybrid LSTM, CNN-LSTM, and Attention-LSTM to predict
the upcoming price to help users make a better choice.
